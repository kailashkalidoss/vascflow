At-home positioning checklist

  • Lie completely flat (supine) — legs uncrossed, arms at heart level.
  • Rest a full 10 minutes before the first BP reading. Do not sit up — stay flat so your ankles and heart are at the same level.
  • Stay calm and quiet — no talking or moving during readings.
  • Use the correct cuff size; arm cuff 1–2 cm above the elbow crease.
  • Ankle cuff on the lower leg just above the malleoli (ankle bones) — no Doppler needed.
  • Take 3 readings per site; the app uses the average.

      Frequently asked questions

      VascFlow FAQ

      What is the ankle-brachial index (ABI)?

      ABI compares blood pressure at the ankle to blood pressure in the arm: ABI = average ankle systolic ÷ higher arm average systolic. In VascFlow it is a self-tracking wellness number, not a diagnosis.

      Why must I lie completely flat for 10 minutes?

      Gravity affects pressure readings. Lying flat keeps your ankles and heart at the same level, and 10 minutes of quiet rest lets circulation settle — so arm and ankle readings are comparable. Do not sit up between readings.

      Why 3 readings at every site?

      Blood pressure fluctuates breath to breath. VascFlow takes 3 systolic readings per site and averages them, which is steadier than any single reading. Wait about a minute between repeats.

      What if one of my 3 readings is way off?

      If a single reading is more than 30% off the others, VascFlow discards it and averages the remaining 2 — the dropped value is shown struck through on the Results screen and PDF. If all 3 readings differ by more than 30%, the set can't be trusted: the app asks you to discard them and collect all 3 readings again.

      Why is the higher arm used for the calculation?

      Arm pressures can differ side to side. Using the higher arm average as the reference is the conservative standard approach — the app identifies which arm was the reference on the Results screen and PDF.

      Automatic or manual — which should I choose?

      Automatic (Omron or similar): the cuff inflates itself and camera scanning is enabled — OCR reads the higher (systolic) number. Manual (sphygmomanometer + stethoscope): scanning is disabled and you type each systolic value by hand.

      Where exactly do the cuffs go?

      Arms: upper-arm cuff 1–2 cm above the elbow crease, arm at heart level. Ankles: one consolidated cuff on the lower leg just above the ankle bones (malleoli). No Doppler probe and no separate foot-vessel readings are needed.

      What do the result categories mean?

      Wellness labels only: Reduced flow (<0.90), Borderline (0.90–0.99), Normal (1.00–1.40), High / stiff (>1.40). They are not diagnostic thresholds. If you have leg pain when walking, non-healing sores, or cold/pale feet, see a clinician promptly.

      Is VascFlow a medical device? Is my data private?

      No — it is wellness tracking only, not a medical device, and not a substitute for professional care. Your readings stay only in this browser on this device; nothing is uploaded to any server. See the full Privacy Policy in the About screen.

      Does it work offline?

      Yes, after the first load — the app, guides, and saved sessions all work offline. Camera OCR needs internet once to download its reading model, and the display font loads from Google Fonts when online (with a system fallback offline).

      How do I view or reprint an older session's report?

      Every entry in Session history (Results screen) and the History popup has its own 📄 PDF button — it regenerates that session's full report (readings, averages, ABI scores, notes) with its original date and session ID, ready to print or save as PDF.

      How do I share results with my health care provider?

      Use Export PDF Report on the Results screen. It includes the disclaimer, date/session ID, all readings with site averages, both ABI scores, your notes, and a signature field — print it or save as PDF to bring to appointments.
